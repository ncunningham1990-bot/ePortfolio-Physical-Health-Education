## How the levels work

Each criterion is marked from 0 to 8 in four bands: **1-2**, **3-4**, **5-6** and **7-8**. The wording moves up the bands in a pattern you can use to improve your work:

| Band | What it looks like |
|---|---|
| **1-2** | Brief points, often as a list, with little detail |
| **3-4** | A basic account that may be incomplete or superficial |
| **5-6** | Detailed and mostly relevant, with some analysis or evaluation |
| **7-8** | In-depth, consistent and justified with reasons and evidence |

Tip: find the band that best matches your draft, then read the band above it and ask, "What would I need to add to get there?"

## Command terms

These words appear in the tasks and rubrics. Make sure you do what each one asks.

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Analyse** | Break down in order to bring out the essential elements or structure. (To identify parts and relationships, and interpret information to reach conclusions.) |
| **Apply** | Use knowledge and understanding in response to a given situation or real circumstances. Use an idea, equation, principle, theory or law in relation to a given problem or issue. |
| **Compare and contrast** | Give an account of the similarities and differences between two (or more) items or situations, referring to both (all) of them throughout. |
| **Demonstrate** | Make clear by reasoning or evidence, illustrating with examples or practical application. |
| **Describe** | Give a detailed account or picture of a situation, event, pattern or process. |
| **Evaluate** | Make an appraisal by weighing up the strengths and limitations. |
| **Explain** | Give a detailed account including reasons or causes. |
| **Identify** | Provide an answer from a number of possibilities. Recognise and state briefly a distinguishing fact or feature. |
| **Investigate** | Observe, study or make a detailed and systematic examination, in order to establish facts and reach new conclusions. |
| **Justify** | Give valid reasons or evidence to support an answer or conclusion. |
| **List** | Give a sequence of brief answers with no explanation. |
| **Outline** | Give a brief account or summary. |
| **State** | Give a specific name, value or other brief answer without explanation or calculation. |
| **Suggest** | Propose a solution, hypothesis or other possible answer. |



# Task 1: Initial Observation and Analysis

**Dubai International Academy, Al Barsha** · Grade 10 MYP PHE · *Criterion A: Knowing and Understanding*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Draft 1 due** | **Friday 5th December** |
| **Length** | 5 pages maximum of written work, plus a separate bibliography |

> **Unit title:** To be or not to be...online
> **Global context:** Identities and Relationships
> **Key concept:** Communication
> **Related concepts:** Environment; Interaction
> **Statement of inquiry:** Communication within a digital environment can influence how identity and relationships are formed.

## What you need to do

Observe and analyse your client's **health** and **physical performance** in your chosen Athletics event, then explain what they need to work on. The checklist below shows exactly what to include.

## Checklist: what to include

### Unit terminology and connections

1. Explain in detail how the e-Portfolio, Athletics, PHE, physical, mental and social well-being, and your chosen sport all relate to the **global context, key concept, related concepts and statement of inquiry**.
2. Use the group Padlet discussion to help you with ideas, along with online sources. **Make sure you reference AI and online sources correctly.**
3. See previous examples for help too.

### Health analysis

4. Write the interview questions you want to ask your client about their health, well-being and fitness. Also complete a **PAR-Q**.
5. Include other information such as average hours of sleep per day, steps per day, screen time, and any other relevant **health** information that could be a useful baseline.
6. Discuss how this was measured (for example ACSI, WHO well-being, CSAI-2, interviews, SMUIS, BSMAS, observation of performance).
7. **Results:** what does this information tell you? Display results in an appropriate format such as tables, graphs, screenshots or illustrations.
8. Relate your findings to the health, diet and sports psychology theory covered in lessons **and beyond**. For example: mental and social well-being, Inverted U Theory, motivation, somatic and cognitive anxiety, arousal, confidence, stress, pressure, diet nutrients, muscle building, sleep and recovery.
9. Analyse these answers and the results of the PAR-Q. Comment on the overall health, fitness and online device use of your client (age, height, weight, BMI, previous injury or illness, recent surgery, diet, calorie intake, water intake, sleep, recovery days, levels of activity, steps and screen time).
10. Refer to **innovative tools** you could use to monitor health, well-being and screen time. Starting points:
    - [20+ Fitness Tools that Track Your Exercise, Meals, Sleep, and More (zapier.com)](https://zapier.com/blog/best-fitness-tracking-apps/)
    - [Technology for Athletic Performance (perch.fit)](https://www.perch.fit/blog/technology-for-athletic-performance)
11. Also ask questions about:
    - relationships (for example coach, client, training partner)
    - how long they spend on devices and social media, and the impact the digital environment has on health and well-being. Use surveys to investigate this (Social Media Use Integration Scale, **SMUIS**, or Bergen Social Media Addiction Scale, **BSMAS**)
    - patterns of movement (understanding and development of skills, techniques and strategies)
    - the use of innovative tools (for example to monitor performance, health or current fitness)
    - self-improvement (for example intrinsic vs extrinsic motivation, anxiety, stress)
12. **Research and reference** to support and validate what you are saying.
13. Evaluate how these aspects and choices could affect the overall performance of your client in their chosen athletic event.

### Physical analysis

14. Introduce and explain the chosen Athletics event. **Reference your sources.**
15. Discuss how the **environment** in which you train and compete influences performance and physical skill (training facilities, relationship with the coach, the environment your coach creates, the digital environment and being online).
16. What components of fitness are needed for this event? Explain and define them.
17. What fitness tests have you conducted, and why?
18. Present the results of relevant baseline **fitness tests** and the **athletic event**. Use **3 trials and the average of each test**. Include a picture of your client performing these and display the results in an appropriate format (tables, graphs).
19. Investigate normative data, world records, national records, DASSA records and so on, and compare your client's results. Present your findings clearly in a table and highlight the range where your client's results fall. **Relate these results to social media influences.**
20. Record and analyse your client's technique in the athletic event. Think about:
    - body position
    - angles of joints (knee, hip, shoulder, elbow, ankle)
    - release angle (if throwing)
    - flight (if jumping or throwing)
    - stride length and stride number (running events)
    - speed of running (jumping and running events)
    - baseline score (time taken to complete the race, or distance of the throw or jump)
    - applying principles like balance, force and coordination

    As part of this analysis:
    - Insert screenshots and explain your client's strengths and weaknesses, comparing them to a "perfect model" (for example an Olympic athlete, world champion or professional).
    - Use PHE terminology (for example muscle names, and joint movements such as flexion and extension).
    - Refer to and use **innovative tools** for your analysis. Apps such as Kinemaster, Kinovea, Hudl and Coach's Eye let you draw angles, lines and circles on images to support what you are saying.

### Summary and next steps

21. What areas do they need to work on? How are the coach-client **relationship**, the **environment** and **communication** going to impact on their performance and athletic **identity**?
22. What will you now do to help them improve and achieve these goals? You will create measurable SMART goals to track progress and develop a performance development plan to support them in their journey.
23. How will you observe their performance and progress to see if they have improved throughout the rest of the ePortfolio? (For example video analysis, questionnaires, feedback meetings, re-testing.)
24. How is your feedback going to be provided? Use a digital environment, for example Padlet.
25. Relate **all of this back** to the MYP key concept, related concepts, global context and statement of inquiry. Put these words in bold or a different colour.

## Structure

- 5 pages maximum of written work.
- Present information in detail and in paragraph form.
- Use tables and graphs to present data and results.
- Use pictures and screenshots to support your work and your analysis of your client's physical and health performance.
- Use appropriate PHE terminology throughout.
- All text in your submitted work must be **no smaller than 10pt**. Screenshots, diagrams, tables and images should follow the same standard so the work is legible on a standard display. Any text smaller than 10pt will not be considered.
- A bibliography must be included in a separate submission.

## Citation

- Have you referenced any information you used from a book or website?
- Use **Harvard** referencing. Place a citation next to the work you have referenced.
- Include a **bibliography** section at the end.

# Task 2: Performance Development Plan

**Dubai International Academy, Al Barsha** · Grade 10 MYP PHE · *Criterion B: Planning for Performance*

> **Global context:** Identities and Relationships
> **Key concept:** Communication
> **Related concepts:** Environment; Interaction
> **Statement of inquiry:** Communication within a digital environment can influence how identity and relationships are formed.

## What you need to do

Build a performance development plan for your client, run it over 8 sessions, check progress halfway, adapt the plan, and reflect after every session. Use the **Task 2 template** from your teacher to guide you, but change the layout of the training programme table to suit you.

## Podcast: a step-by-step guide

Listen to this podcast for a step-by-step breakdown of your PHE ePortfolio.

## Checklist: what to include

### Factors affecting performance

- Discuss how the identified **weak physical aspects** (for example components of fitness such as *power, speed, strength*, and which aspects of the technique or phase of the event) are impacting performance in your chosen Athletics event.
- Discuss how the identified **weak health aspects** (for example lack of protein, lack of sleep, too much social media, lack of confidence, lack of motivation, fear of failure, not being able to perform in front of crowds, anxiety) are impacting performance in your chosen Athletics event.
- This is similar to the previous section in Task 1, so you can put this section together.

### Physical and health goals

- Identify challenging but obtainable goals.
- Develop meaningful goals towards improving performance that go beyond your client's physical development and include growth in physical, social, mental and/or spiritual well-being (Bi):
  - **one physical goal** that will improve your client's performance in the Athletics event
  - **one health goal** that focuses on psychology and will improve your client's performance. Relate it to social media use.
- Set goals in **SMART** format (*Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant and Timely*).
- Explain the relationship between your two goals and how improving them will lead to overall improvement in your Athletics event.

### Training methods

- Define and explain your chosen **physical training method(s)** (for example interval, circuit, weights, plyometric).
- Discuss **technique training**: give examples of specific drills for your Athletics event (**research these**). Explain which aspects of your client's technique need improving (for example take-off, body position) and give examples of exercises that can improve them.
- Define and explain your chosen **psychological skills training method(s)** (for example breathing techniques, visualisation, mental imagery, positive self-talk, muscle relaxation, meditation).
- Within the psychological skills training, explain the **changes to be made to your client's social media use and screen time**, and why and how these changes will affect performance and health.
- Explain why you chose these training methods and psychological skills, and how they will lead to improvements in your client's physical performance, health and well-being.

### FITT and SPORT principles

- Define and explain the principles of training (**SPORT** and **FITT**).
- Explain and give examples of how and why these will be applied to your training programme.

### Innovative tools to track and monitor performance

- Explain and give examples of how you will use innovative tools to track and monitor your client's performance.
- With reference to being "digital": how will you track your client's screen time and social media usage if this is an aspect of change?

### Training programme: sessions 1 to 4

- A structured training plan based on the goals identified.
- Both the health and the physical goals need to be planned for.
- Each session should include a **warm-up, main activity(ies) and cool-down**.
- Each session should also include **psychological skills training**.
- Show logical progression (FITT and SPORT) towards achieving the goals, including drills and activities that practise appropriate techniques and mental well-being.
- Use **innovative tools** to track performance (for example heart rate, workout duration, calories burned, stress levels after psychological skills training, relaxation or meditation apps, diet tracker, sleep tracker).

### Interim performance analysis

- **Re-test the fitness tests and athletic event** halfway through the programme (after 4 sessions) to measure progress.
- Report on progress towards your SMART goals and what changes need to be made to the initial training plan to help your client achieve them.
- Do you need to change your goal?

### Training programme: sessions 5 to 8

- A structured training plan based on the goals identified. **Adaptations should be evident based on the results of the interim performance, with clear examples of changes made from sessions 1-4 to sessions 5-8.**
- Each session should include a warm-up, main activity(ies) and cool-down.
- Each session should also include psychological skills training.
- Show logical progression (FITT and SPORT) towards achieving the goals, including drills and activities that practise appropriate skill techniques and mental well-being.

### Daily reflections

- Include a reflection after each training session on how your client is responding to the training and how your coach-client relationship is developing. Post reflections on your coach-client **Padlet** and take a screenshot as evidence. In the table on the template, summarise your client's reflections from the Padlet.
- Interview your client after each session and record their feedback (voice memo uploaded to Padlet): *What went well? What was difficult? What could be changed for next session?*
- You can give feedback by taking videos of your client during technique training and adding a voice-over. This creates the digital environment between you and your client.
- Report on the effect this programme is having on the health and physical goals.
- Insert screenshots of the **innovative apps** you have used to evidence your client's performance and progress.
- **Analyse** and evaluate progress towards the goals and make modifications to your plan, based on feedback from a discussion with, or observation of, the client.
- **Justify** the changes you will make, or have made, to your original plan.
- What changes do you need to make to your training programme (FITT and SPORT) based on these results?

## Structure

- **5 pages maximum** of written work.
- Font size 10 to 12.
- Use the "Task 2 template" to guide you, **but change the layout of the training programme and table to suit you**.
- Include photos or video links of your client performing. You can analyse these in your reflections to show your knowledge and understanding.
- Use appropriate PHE and MYP terminology throughout.
- Use **bold** and *italics* for words that relate to the **concepts and contexts**.

## Citation

- Have you referenced any information you used from a book or website?
- Use **Harvard** referencing. Place a citation next to the work you have referenced.
- Include a bibliography section at the end.

## The task

- Produce a **3 minute** video showing your performance in your chosen event.
- Show your **final performance** after coaching, demonstrating a much improved performance and application of complex techniques.

## Checklist: what to include

- Video your final performance of your chosen event after **8 coaching sessions**.
- Record your performance from **multiple different angles**, in normal speed and in slow motion.
- To begin, show the **entire performance at full speed on loop**, then show smaller clips of the different phases of the event.
- Include slow motion clips as well.
- **Talk over** your video to explain and justify improvements in your performance. Use PHE terminology.
- **Annotate** on screen using an "innovative tool". Draw angles, lines and circles to emphasise particular aspects of the technique.
- **Focus on and describe** the manner in which skills are performed (for example with accuracy, efficiency, control, coordination, timing, fluency, speed and power), demonstrating **high ability performance**.
- Talk about how you have **incorporated complex strategies, tactics and movement concepts** (for example the Fosbury flop in high jump, the hang or hitch kick in flight during long jump, the crossover step in javelin).
- **Combine complex skills together to achieve higher marks**, for example combining and executing a well-timed run-up approach, take-off and landing with high technique levels in long jump.
- Describe how you have **changed** your technique due to training.
- Include a brief clip of you using some psychological skills training (for example breathing techniques before starting your performance). Comment on the benefits this gave you in your performance.
- Display your **final and initial results** on screen in text format.

## Structure

- The total video must be **no longer than 3 minutes**.
- **No music.**
- It **must** be annotated with audio.
- The video should be well produced with **title slides** for each section. Software like Video Guru, Splice and iMovie is good for this.
- Use tools like Hudl, Kinovea and CoachView to annotate the video.
- Show your performance from a **range of different camera angles**.
- Use slow motion and analysis apps to support your analysis.
- **MP4 format only.**
- Use appropriate PHE terminology throughout.
