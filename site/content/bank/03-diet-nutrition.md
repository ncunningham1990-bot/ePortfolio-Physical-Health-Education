## Nutrients and performance
```written
Level: Evaluate
Q: A teenage athlete says, "I cut out carbohydrates to be healthier." Evaluate this decision.
* Carbohydrates are the body's main and quickest source of energy :: main & (source|fuel) & energy | quickest | energy source | main source | fuel :: What is the main job of carbohydrates?
* Carbohydrate is stored as glycogen in the muscles and liver for exercise :: glycogen | stored | store* :: Where does the body keep carbohydrate for exercise?
* Cutting them out could cause tiredness and poorer performance :: tired* | fatigue* | low energy | poor* performance | worse performance | weak* | run out :: What could happen to training if energy runs low?
* Choose healthier complex carbohydrates (wholegrain, oats) rather than none :: complex | wholegrain | whole grain | oats | brown rice | wholemeal :: Which kinds of carbohydrate are healthier choices?
* Concludes that balance is better than cutting out a nutrient :: balance* | not cut | cutting out | variety | all food groups | disagree | not a good idea :: What is your overall judgement?
Model: I disagree with this decision. Carbohydrates are the main source of energy and are stored as glycogen in the muscles and liver. Cutting them out could cause tiredness and poorer performance in training. It is better to choose healthier complex carbohydrates such as wholegrain bread or oats. A balance of all the food groups is better than cutting out a nutrient, so it is not a good idea.
```

## Energy, labels and choices
```written
Level: Analyse
Q: Explain why a growing teenager who trains six days a week needs more energy than an inactive adult, and what could happen if their energy intake is too low.
* Growth needs extra energy :: grow* :: What is happening to a teenager's body that an inactive adult's body is not doing?
* Training increases energy expenditure (physical activity) :: train* | physical activity | exercise | expenditure | burn* | active :: What does regular exercise do to the amount of energy used?
* BMR plus activity makes up total energy needs :: bmr | basal | at rest | total energy | daily energy :: What are the main parts of daily energy use?
* If intake is too low they may be tired, lose weight, perform worse or risk injury :: tired* | lose weight | weight loss | perform* | injur* | weak* | ill* | growth :: What could happen to performance and health if energy intake is too low?
* Intake should match expenditure (energy balance) :: balance | match* | equal* | same as | enough energy | eat more :: How should energy intake compare with the energy used?
Model: A growing teenager needs extra energy for growth, and training six days a week increases energy expenditure. Their BMR plus their physical activity makes a high total energy need. If their intake is too low they may feel tired, lose weight, perform worse or risk injury. Energy intake should match what they use to keep energy balance.
```
