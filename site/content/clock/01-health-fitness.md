```quiz
Q: Which component of fitness is the ability to keep going for a long time without tiring?
- Flexibility | Flexibility is about the range of movement at a joint, not how long you can keep going.
- Strength | Strength is about the force a muscle can produce in a single effort.
+ Cardiovascular endurance
- Agility | Agility is about changing direction quickly, not lasting a long time.

Q: Which fitness test is the best match for measuring flexibility?
- Bleep test | The bleep test is a running test, so think about which component it measures.
- Grip dynamometer | A dynamometer measures how much force the hand can squeeze.
+ Sit-and-reach
- Vertical jump | A jump test measures leg power, not range of movement.

Q: Which test would you choose to measure muscular strength in the hand and forearm?
- Sit-and-reach | That test looks at the range of movement in the hamstrings and lower back.
+ Grip dynamometer
- Illinois agility run | That is a test of changing direction at speed.
- 12-minute Cooper run | That is a test of how long you can keep running.

Q: Why should a fitness test be done in the same conditions each time?
- It makes the test easier | Standardising a test is about fairness, not about making it easier.
- It makes the scores higher | The aim is accurate scores, and standardising does not change how high they are.
+ So the results can be compared fairly
- So the teacher finishes quicker | Speed is not the reason, the quality of the data is.

Q: What is a baseline in fitness testing?
- The best score in the class | A baseline belongs to one person, not the whole class.
+ A starting score to compare later results with
- A target for the end of the year | A target is a goal, and a baseline is where you start.
- The average score for your age | That describes normative data, which is a different idea.

Q: Which type of data compares your score with the average for people of your age and sex?
- Baseline data | Baseline data is your own starting point.
+ Normative data
- Qualitative data | Think about numbers and tables of averages, not descriptions.
- Target data | Targets are goals you set yourself.

Q: Which unit is VO2 max measured in?
- Beats per minute | That unit measures heart rate.
- Metres per second | That unit measures speed.
+ Millilitres of oxygen per kilogram of body mass per minute
- Kilograms per metre squared | That unit is used for body mass index.

Q: What does VO2 max tell you about a person?
- The strongest weight they can lift | That would be a strength measure, but VO2 max is about oxygen.
+ The most oxygen their body can take in and use
- How flexible their joints are | Flexibility is measured with a different test.
- How fast they can sprint 30 m | Sprint speed is a different component of fitness.

Q: Which is a physical factor that can affect fitness?
- Motivation | Motivation is about thoughts and feelings.
- Friends who exercise | Other people are social factors.
+ Genetics
- Cost of a gym | Money is a social or environmental factor, not a physical one.

Q: In SPORT, what does the S stand for?
+ Specificity
- Stamina | Stamina describes endurance, but it is not one of the SPORT principles.
- Strength | Strength is a component of fitness, not a principle of training.
- Speed | Speed is a component of fitness, not a principle of training.

Q: Training that matches the demands of your sport is an example of which principle?
- Reversibility | Reversibility is about losing fitness when you stop training.
- Tedium | Tedium is about boredom caused by repetitive training.
+ Specificity
- Overload | Overload is about working harder than normal, not matching the sport.

Q: What happens to your fitness gains when you stop training for a long time?
- They get bigger | Think about what the word reversibility tells you.
- They stay exactly the same forever | Fitness is not permanent, so your body does not hold the gains without use.
+ They gradually reverse
- They turn into strength | Fitness does not change into a different component.

Q: In FITT, what does the second T stand for?
- Training | Training is the whole process, but the letter has a more specific meaning.
- Target | That is a goal setting word, not a FITT word.
+ Type
- Tempo | Tempo describes pace, but it is not one of the FITT letters.

Q: Which part of a warm-up gradually raises your heart rate and body temperature?
- Static stretching | Static stretches are usually held still and are more suited to the cool-down.
+ A pulse raiser such as jogging
- A long rest | Resting would lower your heart rate rather than raise it.
- Cooling down | A cool-down comes after exercise.

Q: What is the main purpose of a cool-down?
- To increase your heart rate | You have just done that, so a cool-down does the opposite.
+ To return the body gradually to its resting state
- To build strength | Strength is built by training hard, not by cooling down.
- To test your fitness | Fitness testing is a separate activity.

Q: Which training method uses changes of pace during a continuous run?
- Circuit training | Circuits move between different exercise stations.
+ Fartlek training
- Plyometric training | Plyometrics uses bounding and jumping to build power.
- Weight training | Weight training uses resistance, not running pace.

Q: Which method of training uses bounding and jumping to develop power?
- Continuous training | Continuous training means steady exercise without rests.
- Fartlek training | Fartlek is about speed changes in a run.
+ Plyometric training
- Interval training | Interval training uses work and rest periods, not necessarily jumping.

Q: In the Karvonen method, which two heart rates are used to find the heart rate reserve?
- Resting and average | Average heart rate is not used in this calculation.
+ Maximum and resting
- Target and recovery | Recovery rate is not used in the reserve calculation.
- Average and maximum | The reserve starts from the lowest value, not the average.

Q: How is maximum heart rate estimated?
- 200 minus your age | A common estimate uses a different starting number.
+ 220 minus your age
- 180 minus your age | That figure is too low for most healthy young people.
- 240 minus your age | That figure is too high.

Q: Which is a sign of over-training?
- Improved sleep | Over-training usually has the opposite effect on sleep.
- A lower resting heart rate that stays low | A rising resting heart rate is the warning sign, not a falling one.
+ A resting heart rate that keeps rising
- Feeling full of energy | Persistent tiredness is more typical.
```
