## What you will learn
- Describe mental rehearsal (imagery) and how to use it.
- Describe types of self-talk.
- Explain how routines and cue words help focus.

## Mental rehearsal (imagery)
Mental rehearsal means picturing yourself performing a skill successfully.
- **Internal imagery:** seeing it through your own eyes and feeling the movement.
- **External imagery:** watching yourself like a video.

Tips: use all your senses (sight, sound, feel), imagine the skill going **well**, keep it realistic, and practise regularly. It supports real practice but doesn't replace it.

## Self-talk
Self-talk is what you say to yourself, silently or out loud.
| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| **Positive** | "I've trained for this. I can do it." |
| **Instructional** | "Eyes on the ball." "Elbow up." |
| **Motivational** | "Keep pushing!" |
| **Negative (avoid)** | "I always miss." |

Turn negative thoughts into helpful ones: "Don't mess up" becomes "Focus on a smooth release."

## Focus and routines
- **Cue words** (short trigger words like "smooth" or "explode") bring attention back to the task.
- **Pre-performance routines** (the same steps before a free throw or serve) build consistency and reduce nerves.
- If you get distracted, take a breath, use your cue word and refocus on the next action.
