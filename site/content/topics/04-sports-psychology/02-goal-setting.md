## What you will learn
- Write a SMART goal.
- Distinguish outcome, performance and process goals.

## SMART goals
| Letter | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Specific**: clear and detailed | Improve my 1500 m time |
| **M** | **Measurable**: you can track it | From 8:00 to 7:30 |
| **A** | **Achievable**: challenging but realistic | A 30-second improvement is realistic |
| **R** | **Relevant**: matters to you and your sport | Linked to my team trials |
| **T** | **Time-bound**: has a deadline | Within 8 weeks |

Example SMART goal: *"Improve my 1500 m time from 8:00 to 7:30 within 8 weeks by training three times a week."*

## Types of goals
| Type | Focus | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Outcome** | The result (often depends on others) | Win the tournament |
| **Performance** | Your own standard | Run 1500 m in under 7:30 |
| **Process** | What you do to perform well | Keep my head up and arms relaxed when running |

Performance and process goals are within your control, so they are often more helpful than outcome goals alone.

## Short- and long-term goals
Break one long-term goal into several short-term steps so progress is visible and motivating. Review and adjust goals regularly.
