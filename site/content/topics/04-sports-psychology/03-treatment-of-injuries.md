## What you will learn
- Describe common sports injuries.
- Explain the **RICE** treatment of soft-tissue injuries and newer approaches.
- Know when to seek medical help.

## Common injuries
| Injury | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Sprain** | Damage to a **ligament** (bone to bone) |
| **Strain** | Damage to a **muscle or tendon** |
| **Bruise (contusion)** | Bleeding under the skin after impact |
| **Fracture** | A broken bone |
| **Dislocation** | Bones forced out of position at a joint |
| **Blister / graze** | Damage to the skin surface |

## RICE for soft-tissue injuries
![RICE: Rest, Ice, Compression, Elevation](images/rice-injury-treatment.svg)

| Letter | Action |
|---|---|
| **R**est | Stop and rest the injured part to prevent further damage |
| **I**ce | Apply a cold pack wrapped in a cloth for 10-20 minutes at a time (never directly on the skin) |
| **C**ompression | Support with a firm bandage, not too tight |
| **E**levation | Raise the injured part above heart level to reduce swelling |

Some guides add **P** for **Protect** (PRICE). Newer guidance for soft-tissue injuries is **PEACE** (first days: **P**rotect, **E**levate, **A**void anti-inflammatories, **C**ompress, **E**ducate) and **LOVE** (afterwards: **L**oad gently, **O**ptimism, **V**ascularisation, **E**xercise). Ask your teacher which approach your course uses.

### When to get medical help
Severe pain, obvious deformity, numbness or tingling, being unable to put weight on the injury, a head injury, or no improvement after a few days. **In an emergency, call for help and tell a teacher or adult at once.**
