## What you will learn
- Define arousal, stress and anxiety.
- Describe the inverted-U hypothesis.
- Suggest strategies to manage arousal and anxiety.

## Key terms
- **Arousal:** how alert and physically/mentally activated you feel, from very calm to extremely excited.
- **Stress:** the body's reaction to a demand or pressure.
- **Anxiety:** a feeling of worry or nervousness, often about performance. It can be **cognitive** (racing thoughts, doubts) or **somatic** (physical: butterflies, sweaty hands, fast heartbeat).

## The inverted-U hypothesis
Performance is usually best at a **moderate** level of arousal. Too low (bored, flat) and too high (over-anxious) both hurt performance.

The best level depends on the task:
- **Gross skills** with big movements (rugby tackle, weightlifting) suit **higher** arousal.
- **Fine skills** needing precision (archery, golf putt, free throw) suit **lower** arousal.

## Managing arousal and anxiety
| To calm down | To get fired up |
|---|---|
| Slow, deep breathing | Upbeat music |
| Muscle relaxation | Energetic warm-up |
| Positive self-talk | Motivational cue words |
| Mental imagery of success | Short, sharp activity |
| A consistent pre-performance routine | Team huddle or encouraging words |
