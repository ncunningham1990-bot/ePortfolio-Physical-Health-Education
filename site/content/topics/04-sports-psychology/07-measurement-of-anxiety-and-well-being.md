## Why measure?
We cannot see anxiety directly, so we ask athletes to **rate how they feel**. A questionnaire gives a **baseline** (starting score) that can be compared after training, before a competition, or after trying a technique.

## Examples of questionnaires
| Questionnaire | What it measures |
|---|---|
| **SCAT** (Sport Competition Anxiety Test) | General tendency to feel anxious in competition (trait anxiety) |
| **CSAI-2** (Competitive State Anxiety Inventory-2) | Anxiety and confidence **right before** a competition: **cognitive anxiety, somatic anxiety and self-confidence** |
| **Well-being or mood scales** (for example the WHO-5 well-being index) | Overall mood, energy and well-being over recent weeks |

In these tools you rate statements on a scale, for example from 1 (*not at all*) to 4 (*very much so*):
- *"I am concerned about performing badly."* (cognitive anxiety)
- *"My heart is racing."* (somatic anxiety)
- *"I feel confident I can meet the challenge."* (self-confidence)

### Strengths and limitations
- **Strengths:** quick, cheap, private, and easy to repeat.
- **Limitations:** answers are **self-reported** and can be inaccurate or untruthful; mood changes day to day; wording and scales may not suit everyone; they show scores, not the *cause*.
- They are **not a diagnosis**. If you are worried about your anxiety or mood, talk to a trusted adult, such as a teacher, school counsellor, parent or doctor.
