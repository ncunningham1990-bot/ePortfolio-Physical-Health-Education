## Energy balance
Energy is measured in **kilocalories (kcal)** or **kilojoules (kJ)** (1 kcal ≈ 4.2 kJ).
- **Energy intake (in):** the energy from the food and drink you consume.
- **Energy expenditure (out):** the energy your body uses.

![Energy balance seesaws: balanced, intake above expenditure, expenditure above intake](images/energy-balance.svg)

When energy in equals energy out, body weight stays stable. If you regularly take in more than you use, the extra is stored (mostly as fat). If you use more than you take in, stored energy is used.

Growing teenagers need plenty of energy. Skipping meals or following fad diets is not healthy for young people.

## Where does energy go?
![A stacked bar showing BMR, activity and digestion as shares of daily energy use](images/energy-use.svg)

- **Basal metabolic rate (BMR):** the energy needed to keep you alive at rest (breathing, circulation, repair). It is usually the **largest** share.
- **Physical activity:** the most variable part, so the part you can change most.
- **Digestion:** a small amount of energy is used to process food.

**Estimated energy requirement (EER) = BMR × physical activity level (PAL)**

Energy needs depend on **age, sex, body size and composition, growth, and activity level**. Active athletes need more.

## Reading food labels
| Label feature | What to look for |
|---|---|
| **Serving size** | Check whether numbers are per 100 g or per serving |
| **Energy (kcal / kJ)** | The fuel a serving provides |
| **Sugars** | Lower is better for added sugar |
| **Saturated fat** | Lower is better |
| **Salt / sodium** | Lower is better |
| **Fibre** | Higher is better |
| **Ingredients list** | Listed from **largest to smallest** amount by weight |

Compare **per 100 g** columns to compare two products fairly.
