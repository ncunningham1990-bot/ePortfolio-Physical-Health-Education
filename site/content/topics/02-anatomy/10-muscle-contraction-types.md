## What you will learn
- Define isometric and isotonic contractions.
- Distinguish concentric and eccentric isotonic contractions.
- Give sporting examples.

![Isometric, concentric and eccentric muscle contractions](images/contraction-types.svg)

| Type | What happens to the muscle | Does the joint move? | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Isometric** | Muscle contracts but stays the **same length** | No | Plank, wall sit, holding a handstand, pushing against a wall |
| **Isotonic: concentric** | Muscle **shortens** while contracting | Yes | The upward phase of a bicep curl; driving up from a squat |
| **Isotonic: eccentric** | Muscle **lengthens** while contracting under load | Yes | Lowering the weight in a bicep curl; landing from a jump |

**Isotonic** means the muscle changes length. Concentric and eccentric are the two kinds of isotonic contraction. Eccentric contractions help us **control** a movement and absorb force.

## Try it
```quiz
Q: A gymnast holds a handstand on the beam. The muscles are working but the joints do not move. Which contraction is this?
- Concentric | In a concentric contraction the muscle shortens. Is the joint moving here?
- Eccentric | In an eccentric contraction the muscle lengthens. Is anything moving here?
+ Isometric
H: "Iso-" means same and "metric" relates to length.

Q: The bicep shortens while lifting a weight to the shoulder. What type of contraction is this?
+ Concentric
- Eccentric | Does the muscle get longer or shorter?
- Isometric | Does the muscle length stay the same?

Q: You land from a jump and bend your knees, lowering your body under control. The quadriceps are lengthening while contracting. Which contraction?
- Concentric | The muscle is lengthening, so which word describes a lengthening contraction?
+ Eccentric
- Isometric | Do the knees stay still?
```

Landing from a jump = Isotonic: eccentric | Is the muscle absorbing force by lengthening?
