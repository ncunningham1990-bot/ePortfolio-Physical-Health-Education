## What you will learn
- Describe the five functions of the skeleton.
- Link key bones to the organs or movements they support.
- Explain the difference between ligaments, tendons and cartilage.

## Functions of the skeleton
| Function | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Support** | Gives the body shape and holds it up | The spine keeps you upright |
| **Protection** | Shields soft organs | The cranium protects the brain; ribs protect the heart and lungs |
| **Movement** | Bones act as levers that muscles pull on | The leg bones when you kick |
| **Blood cell production** | Red and white blood cells are made in bone marrow | Marrow in the femur and pelvis |
| **Mineral storage** | Stores calcium and phosphorus | Calcium for strong bones |

An adult skeleton has **206 bones**.

## Key bones
Cranium (skull), vertebrae (spine), ribs, sternum (breastbone), clavicle (collarbone), scapula (shoulder blade), humerus (upper arm), radius and ulna (forearm), pelvis, **femur** (thigh, the longest bone), patella (kneecap), tibia and fibula (lower leg).

## Connective tissue
- **Ligaments** join bone to bone and hold joints together.
- **Tendons** join muscle to bone so muscles can pull on them.
- **Cartilage** covers the ends of bones, cushions joints and reduces friction.
