---
title: Features of a synovial joint
summary: Cartilage, synovial membrane, synovial fluid and ligaments (plus the capsule and bone ends).
---
## What you will learn
- Name the main features of a synovial joint.
- Explain the function of each feature.

![Structure of a synovial joint](images/synovial-joint-labelled.svg)

| Feature | Function |
|---|---|
| **Articular cartilage** | Smooth, slippery layer covering the ends of the bones. Reduces **friction** and absorbs shock |
| **Synovial membrane** | Lines the inside of the joint capsule and **produces synovial fluid** |
| **Synovial fluid** | Fills the joint cavity. **Lubricates** the joint and nourishes the cartilage |
| **Ligaments** | Tough, slightly elastic bands joining **bone to bone**. Hold the joint together and limit unwanted movement |
| **Joint capsule** | A tough sleeve that surrounds the joint and holds the fluid in |

**Tendons** are not part of the joint, but they cross it: they attach **muscle to bone** so that muscles can move the joint.

```label
image: images/synovial-joint-numbered.svg
alt: Synovial joint with six numbered parts to label
1 = Bone | These are the structures at the top and bottom of the diagram, which meet at the joint.
2 = Articular cartilage | The blue layers cover the ends of the bones.
3 = Synovial fluid | The pale yellow space between the bones is filled with this.
4 = Synovial membrane | The orange lining inside the capsule makes the fluid.
5 = Joint capsule | The grey sleeve holds everything in around the joint.
6 = Ligament | The brown bands on the outside join one bone to another.
```
```match
Reduces friction between bones = Articular cartilage | Which feature is a smooth layer on the ends of bones?
Produces synovial fluid = Synovial membrane | Which feature lines the capsule?
Lubricates the joint = Synovial fluid | Which feature is a liquid that reduces friction?
Joins bone to bone = Ligament | Which tissue connects two bones?
Joins muscle to bone = Tendon | Which tissue connects muscle to bone?
```
```quiz
Q: What happens to the joint if there is too little synovial fluid?
- Bones would move more smoothly | Fluid lubricates. What would the lack of lubrication do?
+ There would be more friction and the joint would be stiff or painful
- The ligaments would grow longer | The question is about lubrication, not ligament length.

Q: Which feature absorbs shock and covers the bone ends?
- Ligament | Ligaments hold bones together; they do not cover the bone ends.
+ Articular cartilage
- Tendon | Tendons attach muscle to bone.

Q: A sprain is damage to the tissue that joins bone to bone. Which tissue is this?
- Tendon | A tendon joins muscle to bone, so is that bone to bone?
+ Ligament
- Cartilage | Cartilage cushions the bone ends rather than joining bones together.
```
