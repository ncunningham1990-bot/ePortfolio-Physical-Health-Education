## What you will learn
- Define the **origin** and **insertion** of a muscle.
- Locate the origin and insertion of the biceps.
- Explain how origin and insertion produce movement.

## Definitions
Skeletal muscles are attached to bones by **tendons**.
- **Origin:** the attachment of a muscle to a bone that **does not move** (or moves least) when the muscle contracts. It is usually the end closest to the centre of the body.
- **Insertion:** the attachment to the bone that **moves** when the muscle contracts. It is usually further from the centre of the body.

When a muscle **contracts** (gets shorter), it pulls its **insertion towards its origin**.

![The biceps: origin on the scapula, insertion on the radius](images/origin-insertion-labelled.svg)

### Example: the biceps
- **Origin:** the scapula (shoulder blade).
- **Insertion:** the radius (forearm), by a tendon just below the elbow.
- **Action:** when the biceps contracts, the forearm is pulled up towards the shoulder, so the elbow **flexes**.
