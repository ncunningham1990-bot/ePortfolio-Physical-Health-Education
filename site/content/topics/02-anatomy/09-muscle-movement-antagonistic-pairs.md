## What you will learn
- Explain why muscles work in antagonistic pairs.
- Identify the agonist and antagonist for movements at the elbow, knee and hip.

## Muscles only pull
Muscles can only **pull** (by contracting). They cannot push. So to move a joint in two directions you need **two muscles working in opposite directions**. This is an **antagonistic pair**.
- **Agonist (prime mover):** the muscle that **contracts** to cause the movement.
- **Antagonist:** the muscle that **relaxes** (lengthens) to allow the movement.

When the movement reverses, the roles swap.

![Biceps and triceps as an antagonistic pair at the elbow](images/antagonistic-pair.svg)

| Joint action | Agonist | Antagonist |
|---|---|---|
| Elbow flexion | Bicep | Tricep |
| Elbow extension | Tricep | Bicep |
| Knee flexion | Hamstrings | Quadriceps |
| Knee extension | Quadriceps | Hamstrings |
| Hip flexion | Hip flexors | Gluteus maximus |
| Hip extension | Gluteus maximus | Hip flexors |
| Ankle plantar flexion | Gastrocnemius | Tibialis anterior |
| Ankle dorsiflexion | Tibialis anterior | Gastrocnemius |
