## What you will learn
- Explain the principles of training (**SPORT**).
- Use **FITT** to plan, progress and adjust training.

## SPORT
| Letter | Principle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S** | **Specificity** | Training should match the demands of your sport and the component you want to improve | A swimmer does swimming sets, not only running |
| **P** | **Progression** | Increase the demand gradually so you keep improving and avoid injury | Add a little distance or weight each week |
| **O** | **Overload** | Work harder than you normally do so the body has to adapt | Running further or faster than usual |
| **R** | **Reversibility** | Fitness gains are lost if you stop training | Fitness falls during a long break |
| **T** | **Tedium** | Boredom reduces motivation, so add variety | Mix running, cycling and circuits |

Progression and overload work together as **progressive overload**.

![Fitness rises in steps with progressive overload and falls when training stops](images/chart-overload-reversibility.svg)

## FITT: how to apply overload
| Letter | Stands for | Ways to change it |
|---|---|---|
| **F** | **Frequency** | How often you train (sessions per week) |
| **I** | **Intensity** | How hard you train (speed, weight, % of max heart rate) |
| **T** | **Time** | How long each session lasts |
| **T** | **Type** | The training method (continuous, interval, circuit, weights) |

Change **one** FITT variable at a time and build up gradually. Allow time for **rest and recovery**.

## Try it
```quiz
Q: A runner adds 5 minutes to their long run each week. Which principle is this?
- Reversibility | Reversibility is about losing fitness. Is the runner losing or building?
- Tedium | Tedium is about boredom, which the question does not mention.
+ Progression (progressive overload)
H: The demand is being increased gradually. Which word means "step by step increase"?

Q: After six weeks off with a broken wrist, Sam's fitness has dropped. Which principle explains this?
+ Reversibility
- Specificity | Specificity is about matching training to the sport. Was that the issue?
- Overload | Overload is about working harder. Sam has been resting, so look again.

Q: A footballer only does long slow jogs and finds sprint speed in matches has not improved. Which principle is being ignored?
- Reversibility | The footballer is still training, so is fitness being lost or is training not matching the need?
+ Specificity
- Tedium | Nothing in the question mentions boredom.
H: Is the training matching what the sport actually demands?

Q: A student increases training from 3 days to 4 days a week. Which part of FITT is that?
- Intensity | Intensity is how hard. Is the change about how hard or how often?
+ Frequency
- Time | Time is the length of each session. The sessions are not getting longer in this example.
```

```written
* A suitable FITT change (for example add a session, run a bit faster or longer) :: frequency | intensity | time | type | add a session | three times | faster | longer | 30 minutes :: Which of the FITT letters could Jo change?
* Changes only one thing at a time or increases gradually :: one at a time | one thing | one variable | gradual* | slowly | small :: How should she make the change to avoid doing too much too soon?
* Adds variety to avoid tedium (for example intervals, cycling or swimming) :: tedium | variety | vary | boring | bored | interval* | fartlek | cycling | swimming :: How could Jo make training more interesting?
* Explains that the body adapts and needs rest to recover :: adapt* | recover* | rest | injur* :: Why does Jo need rest as well as harder training?
Model: Jo's demand has not increased, so she needs progressive overload. Using FITT she could add a third session or run a little longer, changing only one thing at a time so the increase is gradual.
```
