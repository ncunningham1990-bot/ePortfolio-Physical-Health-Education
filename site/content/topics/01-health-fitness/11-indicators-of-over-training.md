## What you will learn
- Define over-training.
- Describe physiological and psychological indicators.
- Suggest how to prevent or respond to over-training.

## What is over-training?
**Over-training** happens when the training load is too high and recovery is too short, for too long. Instead of getting fitter, performance **stops improving or falls**. Teenagers who are still growing, who specialise in one sport very early, or who feel pressure from others can be at higher risk.

![Performance rises with balanced training, then falls when training load is too high](images/chart-overtraining.svg)

## Indicators
| Physiological (body) | Psychological (mind) |
|---|---|
| Performance stops improving or drops | Low motivation, loss of enjoyment |
| Constant tiredness and heavy legs | Irritability and mood swings |
| Higher resting heart rate | Anxiety or low mood |
| Slower recovery of heart rate after exercise | Trouble concentrating |
| Frequent illness or infections | Loss of confidence |
| Muscle soreness that does not go away, more injuries | Feeling "burnt out" |
| Poor sleep or loss of appetite | Dreading training |

## Prevention and what to do
- Follow **progression**: increase the load gradually.
- Include **rest days** and quality sleep.
- Eat enough and stay hydrated.
- Vary the type of training (avoids tedium).
- Monitor resting heart rate, sleep and mood.
- If you notice several signs, **reduce the load** and talk to a coach, teacher, parent or doctor.
