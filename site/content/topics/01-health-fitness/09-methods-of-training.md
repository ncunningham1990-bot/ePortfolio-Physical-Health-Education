## What you will learn
- Describe six methods of training.
- Match a method to a component of fitness and a sport.

## The six methods
| Method | What it is | Improves | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Continuous** | Steady exercise at the same pace for a long time (at least about 20 minutes) with no rest | Cardiovascular endurance | A 30-minute jog |
| **Fartlek** | "Speed play": the pace and terrain change throughout the session | Cardiovascular endurance and some speed | A run mixing jogging, sprints and hills |
| **Interval** | Periods of hard work alternating with rest or low-intensity recovery | Speed, power and endurance | 8 × 200 m sprints with a 1-minute rest |
| **Circuit** | A series of exercise stations completed one after another | Muscular endurance, strength and general fitness | 10 stations of 30 seconds each |
| **Weight (resistance)** | Lifting body weight, free weights or machines | Strength (heavy loads, few reps) or muscular endurance (light loads, many reps) | Squats, bench press |
| **Plyometric** | Explosive jumping and bounding exercises | Power | Box jumps, depth jumps |

![Intensity profiles of continuous, interval and fartlek training](images/training-method-profiles.svg)

### Choosing a method
Use the **specificity** principle: match the method to your sport and the component you want to improve. A sprinter might choose interval and plyometric training. A marathon runner might choose continuous and fartlek.
