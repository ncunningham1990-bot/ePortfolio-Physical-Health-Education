## What you will learn
- Match common fitness tests to the component they measure.
- Explain why we test fitness and how to use results.

## Why test?
Testing shows your **strengths and weaknesses**, gives a **starting point** (baseline), lets you **track progress** by retesting, and helps you **set goals** and plan training.

## Common tests
| Test | Component measured |
|---|---|
| Multistage fitness test (bleep test) | Cardiovascular endurance |
| One-minute push-up test | Muscular endurance (upper body) |
| Grip strength dynamometer | Muscular strength |
| Sit-and-reach | Flexibility |
| Standing long jump | Power (legs) |
| 30 m or 40 m sprint | Speed |
| Illinois agility run | Agility |
| Ruler drop test | Reaction time |
| Stork stand | Balance |

## Good testing
- **Reliable:** you get similar results each time under the same conditions.
- **Valid:** the test really measures what it claims to.
- Use the **same test, same conditions** when retesting.
- Compare your result with **normative data** (typical scores for your age and sex), but remember that everybody starts from a different place and improvement matters more than comparison.
- Stop if you feel pain or dizziness.
