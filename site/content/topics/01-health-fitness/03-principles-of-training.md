## What you will learn
- Describe the principles of training.
- Use FITT to plan or change a training programme.

## The principles
| Principle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Specificity** | Training should match the demands of your sport or goal | A swimmer does swimming drills, not only running |
| **Progressive overload** | Gradually increase the demand so the body keeps adapting | Adding a little weight or distance each week |
| **Reversibility** | Fitness gains are lost if you stop training | Losing fitness after an injury break |
| **Variety (avoiding tedium)** | Change activities to stay motivated | Mixing running, cycling and circuits |
| **Individual differences** | People respond differently, so programmes should suit the person | Different starting fitness and goals |

## FITT: how to apply overload
| Letter | Stands for | Ways to change it |
|---|---|---|
| **F** | Frequency | How often you train (days per week) |
| **I** | Intensity | How hard you train (speed, weight, heart rate) |
| **T** | Time | How long each session lasts |
| **T** | Type | The kind of training (continuous, interval, circuit, weights) |

Increase only one FITT element at a time and build up gradually. Doing too much too soon raises the risk of injury.
