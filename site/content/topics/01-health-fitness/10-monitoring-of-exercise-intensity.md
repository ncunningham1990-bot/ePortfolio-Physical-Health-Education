## What you will learn
- Estimate maximum heart rate and describe heart rate training zones.
- Use the **Karvonen method** to calculate a target heart rate.

## Why monitor intensity?
Training must be hard enough to cause adaptation (overload) but not so hard that it causes injury or over-training. **Heart rate** is an easy, useful guide to how hard your body is working.

## Heart rate training zones
**Estimated maximum heart rate (max HR) = 220 − age.** Training zones are a **percentage of max HR**.

![Heart rate training zones for a 15-year-old with an estimated maximum heart rate of 205 bpm](images/hr-training-zones.svg)

Zone names and percentages vary between sources, but in general:
- **Low zones (about 50-70%):** recovery and aerobic base.
- **Middle zone (about 70-80%):** aerobic fitness. This is the usual **aerobic training zone**.
- **High zones (about 80-100%):** anaerobic training and maximal effort. Only for short periods.

You can measure your pulse at the **radial** (wrist) or **carotid** (neck) pulse for 15 seconds and multiply by 4, or use a heart-rate monitor.

## The Karvonen method
The Karvonen method also uses your **resting heart rate**, so it personalises the target to the individual.

**Heart rate reserve (HRR) = max HR − resting HR**

**Target HR = (HRR × intensity %) + resting HR**
