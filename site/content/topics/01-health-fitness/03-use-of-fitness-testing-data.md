## What you will learn
- Explain how and why scores are compared with **normative data**.
- Choose and read graphs and tables to analyse fitness and performance.

## Normative data
**Normative data** are typical scores collected from large groups of people of a similar age and sex. Comparing your score with them tells you whether you are **below average, average or above average** for your group.

Why compare?
- To find strengths and weaknesses.
- To set realistic goals.
- To check whether your fitness is in a healthy range.

But remember: norms are *averages*. Everyone starts from a different place, so **improvement against your own baseline** is often more meaningful than comparing with others.

## Tables and graphs
| Use this | When you want to show |
|---|---|
| **Table** | Exact values, and many results at once |
| **Bar chart** | Comparing separate categories (for example your score against the average for each test) |
| **Line graph** | Change over time (for example progress across weeks) |

### How to read a graph
1. Read the **title** and both **axis labels** (with units).
2. Look at the **scale** and the **key**.
3. Describe the pattern or comparison, using numbers.
4. Explain what it means for fitness and what to do next.
