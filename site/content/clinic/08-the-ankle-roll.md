title: The ankle roll
unit: 4
icon: 🩹
summary: A basketball player rolls an ankle. Choose first aid steps, know when to get help and set a SMART comeback goal.
---
Patient: Ali, 15, school basketball player
Intro: Ali lands on another player's foot and rolls his ankle. It hurts, is already puffy and he can put a little weight on it.

Scene: What is the first thing you do?
+ Stop the game and rest, helping him off the court | Resting stops the injury getting worse and is the first step of RICE.
- Tell him to walk it off | Moving on a fresh injury can cause more damage.
- Massage it hard | Hard massage could increase swelling and pain in a new injury.
- Put a heat pack on it | Heat can increase swelling in the early stage.

Scene: Which steps come next to control swelling?
+ Ice wrapped in a cloth, a firm bandage and raising the ankle above heart level | Ice, compression and elevation all help limit swelling.
- Ice straight on the skin for an hour | Ice should be wrapped, and used for a short time to avoid skin damage.
- A very tight bandage that cuts off circulation | Compression must be firm but not so tight that it limits blood flow.
- Hanging the leg down to improve circulation | Gravity pulls fluid to the ankle, so it should be raised, not lowered.

Scene: When should Ali see a doctor?
+ If there is severe pain, he cannot put weight on it, there is numbness or it is not improving | These are warning signs that the injury could be more serious than a sprain.
- Never, because all injuries heal alone | Some injuries need a professional to check them.
- Only after playing in two more games | Playing on a worsening injury risks making it worse.
- Only if the ankle looks a little pink | A bit of colour is common, so look for the clearer warning signs.

Scene: Ali wants to be back on court for the semi-final. Which goal is the most SMART?
+ Walk without pain in two weeks and be back for training in four weeks, following the physio's plan | It is specific, measurable, time-bound and realistic, and it is linked to professional advice.
- Get better soon | There is no number or deadline, so it is not specific.
- Play in the semi-final no matter what | It is not realistic if the injury has not healed.
- Train twice as hard as everyone else | This is not measurable and could risk re-injury.

Debrief: You stopped the damage, controlled swelling, knew the warning signs for a doctor and gave Ali a goal that was specific, measurable and realistic.
