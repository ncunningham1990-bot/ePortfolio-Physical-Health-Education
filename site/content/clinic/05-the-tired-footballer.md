Patient: Sam, 16, academy footballer
Intro: Sam trains every day and plays at weekends. He has lost weight, feels exhausted by the afternoon and misses breakfast most days.

Scene: What is the most likely issue behind Sam's tiredness and weight loss?
Data: Training: 7 days a week | Breakfast: usually skipped | Weight: dropping
+ His energy intake is lower than the energy he uses | When expenditure is higher than intake, the body uses its stores, so he loses weight and feels drained.
- His energy intake is much higher than the energy he uses | In that case you would expect weight to rise, not fall.
- He is drinking too much water | Drinking water does not explain weight loss and tiredness in this way.
- He needs fewer vitamins | A lack of energy points to energy balance first.

Scene: Which breakfast would best support his training day?
+ Porridge with banana and milk | This gives slow-release carbohydrate for energy and some protein.
- A black coffee only | This gives almost no energy for training.
- A fizzy drink and a sweet | A quick sugar spike followed by a drop is less helpful than steady energy.
- Nothing, to save time | He is already not getting enough energy, so this makes the problem worse.

Scene: What is the best pre-match meal?
+ A carbohydrate-rich meal such as pasta with a little lean protein, 3 to 4 hours before | Carbohydrate tops up energy stores, and eating well beforehand avoids feeling heavy.
- A large fatty meal just before kick-off | Fat digests slowly and can make you sluggish during the game.
- Only a sports drink and nothing else | This does not provide enough sustained fuel.
- Fasting until after the match | He would start the match with low energy stores.

Scene: After a hard training session, which snack helps recovery most?
+ Chicken wrap or milk and a banana within an hour or so | Protein supports muscle repair and carbohydrate refills energy stores.
- Chilli crisps and a fizzy drink | This is high in fat and sugar but offers little for repair.
- Just a glass of water | Rehydration matters, but he also needs nutrients to recover.
- Nothing until dinner | Waiting too long slows down recovery.

Debrief: Sam needed to eat enough to match his energy use, with carbohydrate for fuel and protein to repair muscle, timed around training and matches.
