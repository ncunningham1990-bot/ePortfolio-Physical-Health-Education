Patient: Priya, 16, school football captain
Intro: Priya is the penalty taker for the cup final. In training she scores nearly every time, but before big matches she feels sick and doubts herself.

Scene: Before the match Priya has a racing heart and sweaty palms, and she keeps thinking "I will miss". Which signs are cognitive anxiety?
Data: Racing heart | Sweaty palms | Thinking "I will miss"
+ The negative thoughts, such as "I will miss" | Cognitive anxiety is mental: worry, doubt and negative thinking.
- The racing heart | A racing heart is a physical (somatic) response.
- The sweaty palms | Sweaty palms are a physical (somatic) response.
- Neither, as anxiety is only physical | Anxiety can show up both in the body and in thoughts.

Scene: According to the inverted-U theory, what is her problem likely to be?
+ Arousal may be too high, so her performance falls off the top of the curve | At very high levels of arousal, performance drops because attention and control suffer.
- Arousal is perfectly at the optimal level | Her symptoms suggest she is beyond that point.
- Arousal is far too low | Her racing heart and sweating are signs of high arousal.
- Arousal does not affect penalties | The theory says arousal affects every skill.

Scene: Which two techniques would you teach her?
+ Controlled breathing and imagery of a successful penalty | Breathing lowers arousal, and imagery builds confidence and prepares the skill.
~ Only telling herself not to worry | This is a start, but negative instructions can keep her focused on the worry. Pair it with something more concrete.
- Running hard sprints right before the kick | This would raise arousal further, which is the opposite of what she needs.
- Ignoring the nerves and doing nothing | Without a strategy her anxiety is likely to carry on.

Scene: After eight weeks of practice you give Priya the CSAI-2 again. What would suggest the training worked?
Data: Before: cognitive anxiety 28, confidence 18 | After: ?
+ A lower cognitive anxiety score and a higher self-confidence score | Those changes suggest her worry has fallen and her confidence has grown.
- A higher cognitive anxiety score and a lower confidence score | That would suggest things have not improved.
- No change in any score | That would suggest the training had little effect.
- Only a faster 100 m time | Speed is not what the CSAI-2 measures.

Debrief: Priya's worries were cognitive, her arousal was too high, and breathing plus imagery gave her tools to calm down. The CSAI-2 gave evidence that they worked.
