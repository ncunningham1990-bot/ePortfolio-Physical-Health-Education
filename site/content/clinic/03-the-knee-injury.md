Patient: Leo, 17, school footballer
Intro: Leo planted his foot, twisted, and felt his knee give way. It is swollen and he cannot bend it fully.

Scene: What type of synovial joint is the knee, and what movement does it mainly allow?
+ A hinge joint, mainly bending and straightening | Correct. The knee works mostly like a door hinge, which is why twisting it puts it at risk.
- A ball and socket joint, allowing movement in all directions | That description fits the shoulder and hip. The knee is far more limited.
- A pivot joint, allowing rotation | A pivot joint turns, like the top of the neck. Look at how the knee mainly moves.
- A fixed joint with no movement | Leo can usually bend his knee, so it is a moving joint.

Scene: The doctor suspects a torn ligament. What does a ligament do?
+ Connects bone to bone and helps stabilise the joint | Right. Ligaments hold the bones together, so a tear makes the joint less stable.
- Connects muscle to bone | That is the job of a tendon.
- Covers the bone ends to reduce friction | That is the job of cartilage.
- Produces synovial fluid | That is done by the synovial membrane.

Scene: The scan also shows damage to the cartilage in the knee. Why might this make movement painful?
+ Cartilage normally absorbs shock and reduces friction, so damage can cause pain and stiffness | Yes, without a smooth cushioning surface the bones are more likely to rub and the joint feels sore and stiff.
- Cartilage connects the thigh muscle to the shin | That is a tendon's job, not cartilage.
- Cartilage makes the bones grow longer | That is not what cartilage does in a mature joint.
- Cartilage pumps blood into the knee | The joint does not rely on cartilage to move blood.

Scene: During rehab, Leo strengthens the muscles at the front and back of the thigh. What are these muscles called?
+ Quadriceps and hamstrings, an antagonistic pair | Correct. When one contracts as the agonist the other relaxes as the antagonist, and balanced strength supports the knee.
- Biceps and triceps | These are the antagonistic pair at the elbow.
- Deltoids and trapezius | These are muscles around the shoulder and upper back.
- Gastrocnemius and soleus | These are both calf muscles.

Debrief: The knee is a hinge joint that depends on ligaments and cartilage for stability and smooth movement, and balanced strength in an antagonistic muscle pair helps protect it afterwards.
