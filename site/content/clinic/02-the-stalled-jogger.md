Patient: Jo, 16, school runner
Intro: For three months Jo has jogged for 20 minutes, twice a week, at the same pace. She is bored, and her fitness test scores have stopped improving.

Scene: Which training principle is Jo missing?
Data: Frequency: 2 a week | Time: 20 min | Pace: same each run
+ Progressive overload, because the demand on her body has not increased | Right. Her body has adapted to the same workload, so it has no reason to improve further.
- Reversibility | Reversibility is about losing fitness when training stops. Jo is still training.
- Specificity | Her jogging matches her running goal, so that one is not the main problem.
- Tedium | Her boredom is related, but it is a result rather than the missing training demand.

Scene: Using FITT, what is the best first change?
+ Change one thing, such as adding 5 minutes to each run or one extra session | A small change to a single FITT variable is a manageable way to apply overload.
~ Change everything at once: double the sessions, speed and distance | Overload needs to be progressive. Too much, too soon increases the risk of injury.
- Keep everything and just hope | Without a change in the training demand, there is no reason for her body to adapt.
- Rest for three weeks | Resting more does not add the extra demand she needs.

Scene: Jo is bored of her routine. What would add variety and challenge?
+ Fartlek or interval runs with changes of pace | Changes of pace add challenge and variety, and they help avoid tedium.
- Do the same route in the same order | Repeating exactly the same session is the cause of her boredom.
- Walk instead of run | That would lower the training demand.
- Skip the warm-up to make sessions shorter | Skipping the warm-up increases injury risk and does not help her boredom.

Scene: Jo is 16 and has a resting heart rate of 60 bpm. Using the Karvonen method, what is her target heart rate range for 60% to 80% intensity?
Data: Maximum heart rate: 220 − age | Heart rate reserve: max − resting | Target: (reserve × %) + resting
+ About 146 to 175 bpm | Correct. Maximum heart rate is 204, reserve is 144, so 60% is about 146 and 80% is about 175.
- About 122 to 163 bpm | That is 60% to 80% of her maximum heart rate. Karvonen also uses her resting heart rate.
- About 86 to 115 bpm | That is just the percentages of the reserve. Something still needs to be added.
- About 204 to 220 bpm | That is her maximum heart rate or higher, not a target range.

Debrief: Jo needed progressive overload through a single, small FITT change, some variety to beat tedium, and a personal heart rate zone to guide intensity.
